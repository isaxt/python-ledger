How is this calculated?

Net cash flow = total revenue − total expenses. It adds up every record in the ledger, with no date range. It's a quick look at recorded activity, not a measure of profit or a substitute for accounting advice.

Welcome to your ledger

Think of this ledger as a running record of your business's money. Each entry helps you see what came in, what went out, and where it went.

  • Record money earned. This is revenue: sales, payments from customers.
  • Record money spent. This is an expense: stock, supplies, rent.
  • Organize into categories like Sales or Supplies.
  • Review the picture at the top of the page.

What makes a record

Every entry has five parts:

Type
Is the money coming into the business (revenue) or going out (expense)?
Date
When did it happen?
Description
What was it for?
Category
What kind of income or expense was it?
Amount
How much, in whole dollars.

Reading your dashboard

Total revenue
All the money recorded as coming in.
Total expenses
All the money recorded as going out.
Net cash flow
The difference between the two.

Net cash flow = Total revenue − Total expenses

Example: $1,000 revenue − $650 expenses = $350 net cash flow.

Positive means recorded revenue is higher than recorded expenses. Negative means recorded expenses are higher.

Spending by category adds up expenses by category. Money in and Money out list each entry.

This is a basic overview of recorded activity. It is not a full measure of profit and not a substitute for professional accounting advice.

Make your first entry

Choose Add record, pick Revenue or Expense, fill in the date, description, category and amount, then choose Save record.

You'll see a confirmation, the entry will appear in its list, and the totals update automatically.
